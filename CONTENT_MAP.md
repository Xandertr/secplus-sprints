## Flags and unclear material

- Domain names differ inside the notes. The table of contents says '4.0 - Operations and Incident Response' and '5.0 - Governance, Risk, and Compliance'. The introduction (PDF p.8) says 'Security Operations' and 'Security Program Management and Oversight'. This app uses the introduction's names. (PDF 5, 8)
- Domain weights (12/22/18/28/20%) come from the introduction. The notes point to CompTIA's official objectives for the full list. The app hasn't checked against that external document. (PDF 8)
- The table of contents lists '4.3 - Penetration Testing' twice (printed pp. 65 and 66). In the body it's one section spanning PDF pp. 74-75, treated here as one topic. (PDF 6, 74, 75)
- Under 'Policy Administrator' there's a stray fragment ('address, etc.') that seems to belong to the adaptive identity bullet on PDF p.14 (IP address, etc.). It looks like a layout glitch, so the app doesn't treat it as a separate fact. (PDF 15)
- Blockchain has only a few bullets. The step-by-step process is in a six-panel diagram, so the order activity paraphrases the diagram, which was viewed as an image. (PDF 24)
- These Domain 1 pages contain diagrams. Their labels were read from the text layer, and pages 15, 22, 23 and 24 were also checked as rendered images. (PDF 12, 15, 19, 21, 22, 23, 26)
- These Domain 2 pages contain diagrams (misinformation process, DLL injection, buffer overflow, XSS, fileless virus, worm, pass the hash, session hijacking, CSRF, SSL stripping). Labels were read from the text layer, and pages 31, 40, 46 and 48 were also checked as rendered images. (PDF 31, 32, 35, 40, 44, 46, 47)
- Race condition example: the notes give the 'expected outcome' of two $50 transfers as 'A has $50, B has $150 (or A has $0 and B has $200)', which reads like one transfer vs. two. The bad outcome (A $50, B $200) depends on the stated assumption that deposits are immediate and withdrawals aren't. The order activity follows the notes' steps. (PDF 32)
- Race condition page uses a two-column layout that extracts out of order (Mars rover Spirit and Tesla Pwn2Own bullets interleave). The lesson pairs them by meaning, not by extracted line order. (PDF 32)
- Cloud vulnerabilities quotes two statistics (76% not using MFA on management consoles, 63% of production code unpatched for CVSS 7.0+) without naming a source or year. The app mentions them only as the notes' figures and doesn't quiz on the numbers. (PDF 36)
- Application Attacks (PDF 45-46) repeats the injection, SQL injection, buffer overflow and replay attack bullets from earlier sections word for word. The app covers each once in its own topic and doesn't duplicate questions. (PDF 45)
- Password Attacks: 'A hash example' (SHA-256) and the brute force bullet 'The hash:' have no hash value shown in the text layer or the rendered page. The app doesn't invent an example hash. (PDF 48)
- Mitigation Techniques says auto-update is 'not always the best option' without saying why. The recall card offers a likely reason from the OS vulnerabilities page (patches can break things) and says so. (PDF 50)
- PDF 51 holds the end of 2.5 Hardening Techniques and the start of 3.1 Cloud Infrastructures. Domain 2 activities only use its 2.5 content. (PDF 51)
- PDF 51 starts 3.1 Cloud Infrastructures. The shared responsibility matrix on PDF 52 is a diagram, read as a rendered image: in SaaS the customer manages data, devices and accounts, and identity is shared; PaaS also shares applications and network controls; in IaaS the customer manages everything from the OS up. (PDF 51, 52)
- The High availability bullets appear twice, in 3.1 Other Infrastructure Concepts (PDF 54) and 3.4 Resiliency (PDF 64). The app teaches them in both topics. (PDF 54, 64)
- 3.3 Protecting Data repeats the hashing, masking and tokenization material from 1.4. The app gives it fresh questions with a data-protection angle rather than duplicating Domain 1's. (PDF 63, 64)
- 'Recovery testing' is both its own section (PDF 66) and a sub-heading inside Backups (PDF 67). The app treats the section as the main topic. (PDF 66, 67)
- These Domain 3 pages contain diagrams. Labels were read from the text layer, and pages 52 and 56 were also checked as rendered images. (PDF 52, 54, 56, 57, 58, 61)
- The IPS page's text layer contains stray fragments ('1', '(explicit)') from a diagram. They aren't separate topics. (PDF 57)
- Domain 4 repeats several blocks word for word: Static code analyzers (PDF 72 and 73), Vulnerability scanners (PDF 73 and 79), Data Loss Prevention (PDF 79 and 82), and Security incidents (PDF 89, repeated again on PDF 93 under 5.1). Each is taught in the topic for its section, with different questions. (PDF 72, 73, 79, 82, 89)
- Hardening Targets (PDF 69) repeats SCADA/ICS, RTOS and embedded systems from 3.1, and Wireless Security Settings (PDF 71) repeats the AAA framework from 1.2. The Domain 4 topics ask about them from a hardening and wireless angle. (PDF 69, 71)
- Secure Protocols has a table only in image form (Telnet/SSH, HTTP/HTTPS, IMAP/IMAPS, FTP/SFTP). It was read from the rendered page and turned into a match activity. Apart from this table, PDF 68-105 has no diagrams. (PDF 81)
- Time-of-day restrictions: the example 'R&D databases are only after between 8 AM and 6 PM' looks garbled. It probably means they're only available between 8 AM and 6 PM. The app doesn't quiz on it. (PDF 86)
- Incident Response: the precursor bullets ('An incident might occur in the future', web server log, exploit announcement, direct threats) sit under 'The challenge of detection' with no heading of their own. The app calls them precursors, the word the notes use on PDF 90. (PDF 89)
- Domain 5: text was extracted and mapped to sections, but not yet read in detail or turned into lessons. (PDF 93, 105)
